Privacy Policy — Jyotim

Jyotim ("we", "us") is a devotional app that guides you through pujas, mantra and paath practice, and — if you choose — prepares a personal Vedic astrology (kundli) reading and lets you ask Meera, our AI astrologer, about it.

This policy explains what we collect, why, and what control you have.

1. The short version

Your birth date, time and place never leave your phone.

The birth date, time and place you enter for your kundli, the reading itself, and the puja dedications you set are stored only on your own device, in encrypted form. We do not upload them, and none of our servers hold them. We cannot read them, and neither can anyone who obtains our systems, because we never receive them in the first place. The one exception is Meera's chat, if you use it: to answer you, she receives facts from your reading, such as the doshas it found and the planetary period you are in, but never your birth date, time or place, unless you write them in a message yourself (§2.4).

What does leave your phone: your phone number when you sign in, your name and email if you add them to your profile, a coded form of your phone's device identifier, usage and crash data, tied to your account, a note to Google and Meta when you install the app, sign up, ask for a kundli or subscribe, so we can see which of our ads work, the name of the town you choose for your «Bhakti Bal» card with how much you practised in the last 30 days, so the card can show your place in that town, and, if you use Meera's chat, what you write or say to her, her replies, your gender and facts from your kundli reading, which our servers keep and an AI provider processes to write her replies. Your profile is entirely optional — leave it blank and every part of the app still works. None of it carries your birth date, time or place.

2.3 Advertising and payments

The free version of the app is supported by ads. Our advertising provider uses your device's advertising identifier — a resettable ID that Android provides, not your name and not your birth details — to show ads, to limit how often the same ad repeats, and to show ads chosen for you based on your activity across apps (personalised ads). Where the law requires your consent first, the app asks before doing so. You can switch personalised ads off at any time in the app's Settings — you still see ads, just not personalised ones — and you can reset the identifier or switch them off for your whole device in Settings → Google → Ads. Subscribers to Jyotim Select are shown no ads at all. Meera's plans and question packs do not remove ads.

What the advertising provider receives, and what it does with it. Besides the advertising identifier, our advertising provider receives your device's IP address, which ads you were shown and tapped, and basic technical information about your device and the app. It uses these to serve and measure ads, to work out roughly which area you are in, and to detect fraud and abuse — for its own purposes as well as ours, under its own privacy policy. None of this carries your name, your birth details or your kundli. Subscribers to Jyotim Select are shown no ads, so none of it is sent at all.

Measuring our own ads (Google and Meta). We advertise Jyotim on Google and on Meta (Facebook and Instagram). To learn which of those ads bring people to the app, the app includes Google's measurement tool (Google Analytics for Firebase) and Meta's (the Meta SDK). They receive: that the app was installed and opened, and in general terms how it is used (such as when and for how long it is open); when you sign up, send your birth details for a kundli reading (only that you did, never the details), start an introductory offer or buy a plan, with the plan and its price; your device advertising identifier, a random identifier they create for this installation of the app, your IP address, and basic technical information about your device (such as its model and Android version). Google and Meta use this to measure our ads and to show them to people likely to find Jyotim useful — for their own purposes as well as ours, under their own privacy policies. They do not receive your phone number, your name, your email, your account number, your birth details or your kundli. This measurement runs for Jyotim Select subscribers too. If you switch personalised ads off in the app's Settings, or decline them where the app asks, the app tells Google not to use this data for advertising and stops sending your advertising identifier to Meta; you can also reset or delete the identifier for your whole device in Settings → Google → Ads.

Your birth details and your kundli are never used for advertising, and are never shared with any advertising provider. They cannot be — your birth date, time and place never leave your device, and the reading facts Meera's chat uses go only to our servers and to the AI provider that writes her replies.

When you pay, for Meera's plan, a question pack or Jyotim Select, the payment is handled by the provider you pay through: Google Play, or our payment partner for UPI AutoPay, one-time UPI payments and QR codes, cards and net banking. They receive your payment details; we do not. What reaches us is only whether a payment went through or a subscription is active, which plan or pack it is, when it renews, and which UPI app you paid with — never your card number, UPI ID, or bank credentials. If you pay through our payment partner, we pass it your phone number so that its payment screen does not ask you to type it in, and your random account number so that the payment reaches your account. We do not give it your name, your email or anything else about you. If you pay through Google Play, a purchase service acting on our instructions receives the purchase record Google sends, together with your random account number, and tells us whether your subscription is active or which pack you bought. It receives no phone number, name or email, and no identifier of your device.

2.4 Meera, the AI astrologer

Meera is an AI astrologer inside Jyotim. If you never open her chat, nothing in this section applies to you.

What Meera knows about your kundli. Your birth date, time and place stay on your phone. With each question, your phone sends the facts Meera needs from the reading it made: the goal you are asking about, the doshas and other findings and how strong they are, the planetary periods you are in, what this month and the coming months hold for that goal, the upaay you are doing and how far you have got, the kinds of work your kundli favours, whether your birth time is known, and the app's language. These facts are used for that question and are not stored on our servers.

Your messages. What you write to Meera, or say to her using the microphone, and her replies are stored on our servers in India, linked to your account, so the chat is there when you come back and Meera can follow the conversation. When your kundli changes (you edit your birth details, or a reinstall or a new phone has different ones), a new chat starts. The earlier one stays readable in the app, but Meera no longer sees it. Messages are deleted 12 months after a chat's last message, and at once if you delete your account (§5, §6).

Anything you write is kept as you wrote it. The chat does not hide dates, names or other details in your messages. Meera does not need anyone else's personal details. Please do not share another person's details, such as a family member's birth details, unless you have their permission.

Your gender and your situation. At the start of a chat, Meera asks with one tap whether you are a woman or a man. Hindi changes its words with the person spoken to, so she needs this to speak to you correctly and to understand your family situation. It does not change your kundli reading. She also asks which situation fits you for that goal. Both are kept with your account until your kundli changes (then she asks again) or you delete your account. You can change your answer in the chat.

A code that tells one kundli from another. So that a chat is never carried over to someone else's kundli, your phone works out a short code from your birth details and sends it with the chat. We use it only to see whether the birth details behind your chat have changed. The code is deliberately short: millions of different birth details share the same code, so it cannot be turned back into yours. The app never sends your birth details themselves to our servers.

Speaking instead of typing. If you tap the microphone, the app asks for permission to use it. Your phone's own speech service (on most Android phones, Google's) turns what you say into text in the message box, and may send the sound to Google to do so, under Google's privacy policy. Jyotim does not save the sound of your voice and never sends it to our servers. Nothing goes to Meera until you press send, and then only the words.

Hearing a reply. If you tap «सुनिए» under one of Meera's replies, your phone reads it aloud in Google's Hindi voice, which works online: the words of that reply are sent to Google to be spoken, under Google's privacy policy. If that voice is not available, a voice on your phone reads it without sending it anywhere.

Reporting a reply. If you tap «रिपोर्ट करें» under a reply, we keep the exact reply, the reason you chose and any note you add with your account, and email a copy to our support mailbox (support@jyotim.com, run on Google Workspace) so we can look into it. The email carries the reply, the reason, your note, the goal and the chat's language, never your phone number or your account number, and long numbers in it are masked. The copy kept with your account is deleted with the reply it is about. The email is kept for up to 12 months.

Who writes Meera's replies. An AI model from our AI service provider writes them, acting on our instructions. With each question, our server sends the provider your question, the earlier messages of that chat and the reading facts above. The provider does not receive your phone number, your name, your email or your account number, and it processes the data on servers outside India. Under its commercial terms it may not use this data to train its models, and it deletes it within 30 days, except that it may keep a message its safety systems flag as breaking its usage rules for up to 2 years, or longer where the law requires.

Who at Jyotim reads your chat. Nobody at Jyotim reads your chats as a matter of course. We read a chat only when you report a reply or write to us about it, or when we must look into a fault or a misuse of the service. We do not use your chats to train AI models.

Paying for Meera. How payments work is described in §2.3. No Meera plan or pack removes advertising.

3. Signing in with your phone number

To use Jyotim you sign in with your mobile number. We send a one-time code to that number by SMS — or on WhatsApp, if the SMS cannot be delivered; entering the code signs you in.

What we store. Your phone number, on our servers in India, linked to a random account identifier — together with whatever you have chosen to put in your profile. You can change or clear any profile field at any time in the app. Everything else in the app — and our analytics — uses that identifier, never the number itself. Your number is not sent to our analytics or advertising providers. Beyond the messaging provider described below, the only other party that receives it is our payment partner, and only if you pay through it (§2.3).

Why we ask for it. To sign you in, and to make sure each introductory offer is taken once per person. We do not send you marketing messages on it without asking you first.

Who else sees it. The SMS carrying your code — or, if it cannot be delivered, the WhatsApp message — is sent through a messaging provider acting on our instructions (a WhatsApp message is delivered by WhatsApp). They receive your number only to deliver that message.

How long we keep it. For as long as your account exists. Deleting your account (Section 6) deletes it — apart from the coded markers described in Section 5.

4. Why we are allowed to process this (legal basis)

Under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDP Act), we process your personal data on the basis of your consent, which you give by entering the details yourself — your phone number when you sign in, your birth details when you ask for a kundli, and your messages, your situation and your gender when you use Meera's chat. You are told what each field is for at the point where you enter it. You may withdraw consent at any time by deleting your data (Section 6 below) or by uninstalling the app.

5. How long we keep it

Your birth date, time and place, your kundli reading and your practice record live only on your own device, so you control their retention. They are kept until you delete them in the app or uninstall the app.

Your phone number, your profile and your account are kept for as long as your account exists, and deleted when you delete your account.

Meera's chat. Your messages and Meera's replies are deleted 12 months after the chat's last message, together with any report about them. Your gender, your situation and the code described in §2.4 are kept with your account until your kundli changes or you delete your account. A record of each question answered (when it was answered and how it was paid for, never its words) is kept for as long as your account exists. Deleting your account deletes all of it at once. A report emailed to our support mailbox is kept there for up to 12 months.

Coded markers are kept after deletion — only if you took an introductory offer. An offer can be taken once per person and once per device, so that a deleted account cannot claim it a second time we keep a coded record of the phone number and of the devices that account used. They cannot be turned back into your phone number or your device's identifier, they hold nothing else about you, they are never shared, and they are used for nothing else.

A subscription still running after deletion. Deleting your account does not cancel a payment mandate, Meera's autopay included. Unused question packs are deleted with the account. While such a subscription is still running, we keep a coded link between your phone number and it, so that if you sign in again with the same number, what you are paying for works again. It holds nothing else about you.

Usage and crash data are retained by our providers under their own retention schedules — currently up to 12 months for usage events and up to 90 days for crash reports. When you delete your account, we permanently remove the link between you and that data, so whatever is still held cannot be traced back to you.

What Google and Meta receive to measure our ads (§2.3) is kept by them under their own policies. It was never linked to your account, your name or your phone number, so deleting your account does not reach it.

The notification delivery address (§2.2) is kept by Google under its own policies and stops working when you uninstall the app. Deleting your data in the app removes the goals it follows.

6. Your rights, and how to actually use them

Under the DPDP Act you have the right to access, correct, and erase your personal data, to grievance redressal, and to nominate someone to exercise these rights on your behalf.

  • See or correct your data: your name and email are in the app under Profile → Personal Details, and your birth details can be corrected from the kundli screen. Changing them there updates them everywhere we hold them. Your Meera chats are in the chat itself, where you can also change your gender answer.
  • Delete your account and data: open the app → Settings → Mera data delete karein. This deletes your account, your phone number, your profile and your Meera chats, gender and unused question packs from our systems, and permanently unlinks your usage and crash data so it can no longer be connected to you. On your phone it removes your birth details, profile, sankalp, practice history and reminders, cancels any scheduled reminders, and destroys the encryption key so that anything remaining is permanently unreadable. It cannot be undone.
  • Uninstalling the app removes everything held on your phone — but it does not delete your account. To delete the account itself, use Delete my data before uninstalling, or write to support@jyotim.com afterwards (the steps are at jyotim.com/delete-account).
  • Usage and crash data are linked to your account for as long as it exists. When you delete your account, we permanently delete that link. What remains cannot be connected back to you — not by us, and not by anyone else — and survives only inside overall totals, in the way a completed survey response does. You can ask us about your data at any time at support@jyotim.com.

7. Children

Jyotim is meant for adults. We do not ask your age when you sign in. Under India's DPDP Act anyone under 18 is a child, and a child's personal data may be processed only with a parent's or guardian's consent — so if you are under 18, please use Jyotim only with a parent's or guardian's permission.

The kundli reading checks again. It is the one part of the app that asks for a birth date, and if the date entered shows the person is under 18, the reading is not generated and the details are not saved.

If you believe a child is using an account without a parent's or guardian's consent, contact us at support@jyotim.com and we will delete that account.

8. Security

Personal data on your device is encrypted.

Your phone number and your profile are held on our servers in India, restricted so that only the sign-in system can read them, and never written into logs or analytics. Meera's chats are held on our servers in India too, readable only by our chat system.

No system is perfectly secure, and we will not claim otherwise. What we can say is that we hold as little as possible: your birth date, time and place are never sent to us by the app, so no breach of ours can expose them. Only what you choose to write in Meera's chat reaches us.

9. Changes to this policy

If we change what we collect or how we use it — for example if we start keeping something new on our servers, or if we add a new provider — we will update this policy and the "Last updated" date above, and where the change is significant we will tell you inside the app.
